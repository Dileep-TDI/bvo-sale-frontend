# How to increase the value of your business before you sell

If you plan to sell in the next few years, the work you do now can make a bigger difference to your result than anything you negotiate at the end. The good news is that the things that drive value are mostly within your control.

### How value is worked out

Most businesses are valued on their sustainable earnings, multiplied by a figure that reflects risk. That gives you two levers to pull: lift the earnings a buyer will believe, and lower the risk so they pay a higher multiple. There is also a third: don't leak value at the finish line.

As a simple illustration, a business with $700,000 of normalised EBITDA valued at a multiple of 3 is worth $2.1 million. Lift earnings to $800,000 and reduce risk enough to justify a multiple of 3.5, and it is worth $2.8 million.

### Lever 1: Lift the earnings buyers will believe

- **Clean up the books.** Take personal expenses out so the true profit is clear and needs fewer add-backs.
- **Review your pricing.** Many owners haven't lifted prices in line with costs. Small increases can have a big effect on profit.
- **Benchmark your costs.** Compare wages, rent and other costs with similar businesses to find where margin is leaking.
- **Build recurring revenue.** Contracts, subscriptions and service agreements are worth more to a buyer than one-off sales.
- **Drop work that doesn't pay.** Unprofitable customers or product lines drag down margins.
- **Show a steady trend.** Three years of stable or growing earnings is worth more than one great year.

### Lever 2: Lower the risk to lift the multiple

- **Reduce owner reliance.** Delegate key relationships and decisions, and build up a second-in-charge.
- **Spread customer risk.** Grow your customer base so no single client carries the business.
- **Lock in contracts.** Extend the lease, and put key customers and suppliers on written agreements.
- **Strengthen the team.** Keep key staff and share critical knowledge.
- **Document your systems.** Make the business easy to hand over.

### Lever 3: Don't leak value at the finish line

- **Separate surplus assets.** Property, spare cash and excess stock should be dealt with outside the business price.
- **Get the structure right.** Work through asset or share sale, and the small business CGT concessions, with your accountant. CGT rules change for sales on or after 1 July 2027.
- **Prepare for due diligence.** Have contracts, leases, financials and employee records ready before buyers ask.
- **Choose the right broker.** One who understands value as well as the market will defend your price.

### What not to do before a sale

- **Don't make big capital purchases** just before selling. They rarely add to the price and can reduce the goodwill a buyer pays for.
- **Don't cut costs that drive growth,** such as marketing or key staff, just to lift short-term profit. Buyers notice.
- **Don't rely on aggressive add-backs.** If you can't prove them, buyers won't accept them.

### Frequently asked questions

**How long does it take to increase business value?**
Quick wins like pricing and cleaning up the books can show within months. Reducing risk usually takes one to three years.

**What adds the most value before a sale?**
For most small and medium businesses, reducing owner reliance and showing steady, provable earnings make the biggest difference.

**Should I spend money on the business before selling?**
Spend on things that lift sustainable earnings or reduce risk. Avoid major capital purchases that a buyer won't pay extra for.
