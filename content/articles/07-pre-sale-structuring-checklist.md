# Pre-sale structuring checklist for accountants

The structure a client sells through can matter as much as the price. Getting it right before the business goes to market protects the after-tax result and avoids awkward renegotiation in due diligence. This checklist covers the questions to work through with a client before they sign a broker agreement.

### 1. Separate the business from the entity

The business is the operating enterprise a buyer wants. The entity is the company or trust that owns it, and it often holds much more. Before setting a price, identify:

- **Surplus assets** such as property, investments or excess cash that a buyer won't pay for as part of the business
- **Non-operating liabilities**, including related-party loans and amounts owed to or by directors
- **Division 7A loans** and other shareholder arrangements that need to be cleared or settled
- **Existing goodwill** on the balance sheet, which is not the same as the goodwill a buyer will pay for

A clean line between business and entity assets makes the valuation clearer and the sale easier.

### 2. Decide between an asset sale and a share sale

In an asset sale, the buyer takes specific business assets and the entity stays with the seller. In a share sale, the buyer acquires the company itself, including its history and liabilities.

Things to weigh up include:

- which structure gives the client the best tax outcome, including access to concessions
- whether contracts, licences or permits can be transferred, or are tied to the entity
- the buyer's appetite for taking on historical risk, and the warranties and indemnities they will ask for
- state duty on the transfer of certain assets

Buyers often prefer asset sales and sellers often prefer share sales, so the answer frequently comes down to negotiation.

### 3. Check eligibility for the small business CGT concessions

The four small business CGT concessions remain available and can reduce, defer or eliminate tax on the sale of an active business. Confirm early whether the client meets the basic conditions, such as the $2 million aggregated turnover test or the $6 million maximum net asset value test, and the active asset test.

From 1 July 2027, the 50% active asset reduction becomes available to businesses with aggregated turnover up to $10 million, up from $2 million. The higher threshold applies only to that concession, not to the other three.

### 4. Model the timing around 1 July 2027

For CGT events on or after 1 July 2027, the 50% CGT discount for individuals, trusts and partnerships is replaced with cost base indexation, and a 30% minimum tax applies to the post-reform portion of capital gains. For clients who are close to a sale, model the after-tax result for a sale before and after that date, including the effect of any concessions.

### 5. Watch the proposed trust changes, but don't rush

A 30% minimum tax on certain discretionary trusts has been proposed from 1 July 2028, with rollover relief for eligible restructures. As at late October 2026, the bills are still before Parliament and a Senate committee report is due on 13 November 2026. Many family businesses sit in discretionary trusts, so flag it with affected clients, but avoid restructuring until the final rules are settled.

### 6. Tidy up the operational details buyers will test

- employee entitlements, including leave balances and how they will be treated at settlement
- whether GST going concern treatment will apply
- leases, key contracts and change-of-control clauses
- any restraint of trade the client will be expected to accept
- earn-outs or deferred payments, and how they will be structured and taxed

### 7. Get the valuation and the structure working together

The valuation should reflect what is actually being sold, and the structure should reflect how it is being sold. When the valuer, the accountant and the broker work on the same assumptions, the asking price holds up and the client knows what they will keep.
