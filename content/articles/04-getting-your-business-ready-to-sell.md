# Getting your business ready to sell: what to fix in the 12 months before market

The best sales are engineered well before the business is listed. Buyers will look for every reason to lower the price, so the aim is simple: find those reasons first and deal with them. Here is what to work on in the 12 months before you go to market.

### 12 months out: know where you stand

1. **Get a valuation.** Find out what the business is worth today and, more importantly, what is holding the price back. This gives you a to-do list ranked by what each issue is costing you.
2. **Review your structure with your accountant.** Look at whether an asset sale or share sale suits you, what sits in the entity that shouldn't go with the business, and whether you qualify for the small business CGT concessions. With CGT rules changing from 1 July 2027, timing deserves a conversation too.
3. **Set your goals.** Decide on your ideal timing, how long you would stay on after the sale and whether you would consider deferred payments.

### 9 months out: clean up the numbers

1. **Take personal expenses out of the business.** Every private cost run through the books is an add-back a buyer has to take on trust. Fewer add-backs means a cleaner, more believable profit.
2. **Make sure three years of financial statements are ready.** Buyers and lenders will ask for them early.
3. **Reconcile regularly.** Up-to-date management accounts show a business that is under control.
4. **Deal with tax debts and compliance.** Outstanding ATO debts, missing lodgements or licensing gaps will surface in due diligence.

### 6 months out: reduce the risks buyers price in

1. **Reduce owner reliance.** Document how the business runs, delegate key relationships and build up a second-in-charge. If the business can run for a month without you, it is worth more.
2. **Spread customer risk.** If one or two clients make up a large share of revenue, work on broadening the base or putting those clients on written agreements.
3. **Lock in the lease.** A short lease with no option to renew is a major risk for a buyer. Talk to your landlord about extending it.
4. **Secure suppliers and key staff.** Written supply agreements and retention arrangements for key people give buyers confidence.
5. **Right-size your stock and assets.** Excess stock or equipment that the earnings don't justify can drag goodwill down rather than add to the price.

### 3 months out: get market-ready

1. **Hold off on big capital purchases.** Buying major new assets just before a sale rarely adds to the price and can reduce the goodwill a buyer pays for.
2. **Gather your documents.** Leases, contracts, employee records, licences, insurances and asset registers should be easy to find.
3. **Choose your broker and agree the plan.** Settle the asking price, the marketing approach and how buyers will be qualified.
4. **Keep trading strongly.** Buyers look at the most recent months closely. A dip in performance during the sale can cost you more than anything else.

### Start earlier if you can

Twelve months is a good minimum, but owners who start two or three years out have time to fix the bigger issues, like owner reliance, and to show a track record of improvement. The most valuable business is one that is ready to sell any day.
