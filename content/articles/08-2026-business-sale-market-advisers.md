# The 2026 business sale market: what advisers should be telling clients now

Business-owner clients are hearing a lot right now: a hot market for good businesses, rising interest rates and the biggest change to capital gains tax in a generation. Many will turn to their accountant or adviser to make sense of it. Here is what is happening, and five conversations worth having before the end of the financial year.

### Where the market sits

**Demand is strong and moving up the value scale.** SEEK Business reported that buyer enquiries grew 11% across 2025 and have more than doubled since 2019. Businesses valued above $3 million recorded the fastest enquiry growth, and corporate buyers and M&A advisers are increasingly active.

**Buyers are more selective.** Higher costs have made buyers test earnings more closely. Brokers report more conservative offers, longer finance clauses and longer timeframes to reach unconditional contracts. Deferred payments and vendor finance are appearing more often in deals above $1 million.

**Finance is dearer.** The Reserve Bank lifted the cash rate to 4.60% on 29 September 2026, the highest level since November 2011. That flows through to what buyers can borrow and how they structure offers.

**Tax rules are changing.** From 1 July 2027, the 50% CGT discount for individuals and trusts is replaced with cost base indexation plus a 30% minimum tax on capital gains. The four small business CGT concessions remain, and the 50% active asset reduction extends to businesses with turnover up to $10 million. A separate minimum tax on discretionary trusts, proposed from 1 July 2028, is still before Parliament.

### Five conversations to have now

1. **The timing question.** For clients who may sell in the next two years, model the after-tax result for a sale before and after 1 July 2027, including any concessions. For some, timing will make a real difference. For others, it won't, and they shouldn't rush.
2. **The concessions check.** Confirm which clients meet the conditions for the small business CGT concessions, and which may now qualify for the 50% active asset reduction under the higher turnover threshold. Small changes before a sale can protect eligibility.
3. **The trust structure conversation.** Many family businesses sit in discretionary trusts. Flag the proposed minimum tax with affected clients, but advise against restructuring until the legislation is final.
4. **The value conversation.** In a selective market, buyers pay for clean financials, low owner reliance and secure contracts. A valuation now shows clients what is holding their price back while there is still time to fix it.
5. **The deal structure conversation.** With finance tighter, clients should expect offers that include deferred payments, earn-outs or vendor finance. Help them understand the risk and tax treatment of each before an offer lands.

### Why advisers matter more in this market

When markets are this selective, the quality of the numbers decides the outcome. Accountants are best placed to clean up the financials, sort out structure and get ahead of the questions buyers will ask. Pairing that with a valuer and a broker who understand value gives clients the best chance of a strong result.
