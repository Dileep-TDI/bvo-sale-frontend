# Selling a business in 2026: what today's market means for owners

The Australian market for buying and selling businesses is busy, but it is not easy. Buyers are out there and many have money to spend. They are also asking harder questions than they were a few years ago. Here is what is shaping the market in 2026 and what it means if you are thinking of selling.

### Buyer demand is strong, especially for larger businesses

Interest in buying a business keeps growing. SEEK Business reported that buyer enquiries grew 11% across 2025 and have more than doubled since 2019. The strongest growth was for businesses valued above $3 million, while those in the $1 million to $3 million range also saw enquiry growth above 20%.

The type of buyer is changing too. Alongside traditional owner-operators, corporate buyers and M&A advisers are increasingly active, particularly in industries such as manufacturing, transport and logistics.

That is good news for well-run, established businesses. More serious buyers means more competition for the right opportunity.

### But buyers are more selective

Higher wages, rent and operating costs have made buyers more careful. Brokers report that buyers are testing earnings more closely, asking more questions and structuring deals with more discipline.

In practice, that means:

- add-backs that can't be proven are being rejected
- businesses with unclear or inconsistent financials are sitting on the market longer
- overpriced listings are attracting enquiries but few formal offers

### Finance costs are shaping deals

The Reserve Bank raised the cash rate to 4.60% on 29 September 2026, its highest level since November 2011. Higher borrowing costs affect what buyers can afford to pay and how they structure offers.

Brokers are seeing longer finance clauses and longer timeframes to reach unconditional contracts. Deferred payments and vendor finance are also appearing more often in deals above $1 million. As a seller, you should be ready to discuss structure, not just price.

### Capital gains tax is changing from 1 July 2027

The biggest change for many owners is tax. Legislation passed in June 2026 replaces the 50% CGT discount for individuals and trusts with cost base indexation, plus a 30% minimum tax on capital gains. It applies to gains made on or after 1 July 2027.

For business owners, two points matter most:

- **The four small business CGT concessions remain.** These can reduce, defer or even eliminate tax on the sale of an active business for owners who qualify.
- **The 50% active asset reduction is being extended.** From 1 July 2027, it becomes available to businesses with turnover of up to $10 million, up from $2 million.

A separate 30% minimum tax on discretionary trusts has been proposed from 1 July 2028 and is still being worked through. If your business sits in a family trust, keep an eye on it and avoid rushed restructures.

The practical takeaway is that the timing and structure of a sale now matter more than ever. Ask your accountant to model your position for a sale before 1 July 2027 and after it.

### What this means for you

The market rewards preparation. Businesses with clean financials, realistic pricing and a clear story are attracting serious buyers. Those that are not ready tend to stall.

If you are thinking of selling in the next one to three years:

1. Get a proper valuation now so you know where you stand.
2. Talk to your accountant about structure and the 1 July 2027 tax changes.
3. Fix the issues buyers will discount you for, starting with owner reliance and messy books.
4. Choose a broker who understands value as well as the market.
